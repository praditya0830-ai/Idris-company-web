R&D teams spend weeks drawing one concept, simulating it and starting again. Idris moves the calculation to the beginning, so engineers compare several sized and checked designs before they commit to one.

Method

Language in. Engineering out.

A language model reads the request and explains the result. It never produces a number. Every number comes from a deterministic solver built on a published standard.

  1. 1

    Describe

    Loads, speeds, space and materials, in plain words. Idris asks when a requirement can be read two ways, and shows what each reading changes.

  2. 2

    Generate

    Gears, shafts, bearings, housing, fasteners and oil are sized together. Variants that cannot be made or assembled are discarded.

  3. 3

    Analyse

    3D stress, natural frequencies against running speed, oil temperature and an automatic design review, on any variant.

  4. 4

    Decide

    The engineer chooses. Idris exports the chosen design and never changes it silently.

When a check fails

Idris shows the failure as it is, explains the cause, and proposes computed fixes for the engineer to accept or reject.

Outputs

Complete enough to build.

Every variant is a full assembly with the parts a workshop needs, not a concept sketch.

3D assembly

STEP · STL

Housing with central web and bolted covers, gears, shafts, bearings, seals, fasteners and plugs as closed solids.

Working drawings

ISO 286 · ISO 2768

Housing, cover and shaft sheets with sections, bearing-seat fits and a general-tolerance note.

Bill of materials

CSV

Every part with quantity, specification and mass, including catalogue bearings and standard fasteners.

Gear and shaft sizing

ISO 6336 · SHIGLEY

Tooth bending and contact safety factors, shaft fatigue and bearing selection with seats.

Bearings and lubrication

ISO 281

Rating life for every bearing, and an oil grade chosen so each one reaches its target life.

Bolted joints

VDI 2230 · ISO 898-1

Cover bolt size and count, dowels and O-ring cover thickness from a bolted-joint calculation.

Structural and modal analysis

3D FEM

Stress under bearing loads with margin of safety, and natural frequencies against the running speed.

Losses and temperature

ISO/TR 14179-2

Gear, bearing and seal power losses and the oil temperature they produce.

Verification

Every result can be traced.

A method enters Idris only after it reproduces a published reference case. The live server reruns every benchmark on request, and each result records the solver version, inputs and assumptions.

About IDRIS AI

IDRIS AI is an AI assistant for mechanical and aerospace engineers. An engineer describes a design problem in plain words and receives several design candidates, each scored against engineering calculations the engineer chooses. The AI interprets intent; every number comes from a deterministic solver built on published standards (for example ISO 6336 for gears and ISO 281 for bearings). The MVP is live for VTOL gearboxes, and we are building toward more domains step by step.

How we work

Four habits.

AI is part of the daily workflow.

We use AI coding assistants and LLMs to write, test and review code, draft documents and explore options. We expect you to use them well, and to review what they produce. The person is responsible for the result, not the tool.

Numbers must be true.

Every result carries an evidence level and its limits. We benchmark against published reference cases. We do not claim more than we can show.

Small team, real ownership.

You will wear more than one hat, decide quickly, and see your work used by real engineers.

Written first.

We keep decisions and specs in shared documents so that people and AI tools work from the same source.

This is a startup

We are early-stage. Priorities change, roles are broader than a job title, and some things are still unfinished. You will not find large teams, long processes or a finished playbook. If you want to build something from a working start and are comfortable with ambiguity, this is a good place. Compensation and equity are discussed directly and confirmed with legal counsel.

Founding Computational Engineer / CTOCo-founder track

What you will own

The technical direction of IDRIS and every number it produces: solver architecture, standards-based calculations, benchmarks, and the team that builds them. You work directly with the founder, design-partner engineers and advisors.

What you can do

  • Build solvers, not only use them. You have written or extended analysis code yourself (for example finite element, finite volume or other simulation code, or standards-based calculation tools), and you can show how you validated it against benchmarks or published results. You can turn a standard or a numerical method into tested, reproducible code and document its limits.
  • Computational engineering across the whole CAE field, not only one corner: FEA (meshing, contact, boundary conditions, convergence, modal, thermal, fatigue, nonlinear), CFD (turbulence models, heat transfer, aerodynamics, meshing for flow), multibody and coupled problems (thermal-structural, fluid-structure), and optimization. You know how each method works, what it assumes, and when a result misleads.
  • Breadth to cover many engineering fields over time (machine elements, aerospace structures and aerodynamics, vehicles, piping and thermal systems, marine), with real depth in at least one, and the ability to learn and validate a new field against benchmarks before we open it.
  • Mechanical design calculations: gears, shafts, bearings and joints according to standards such as ISO 6336 and ISO 281, and the ability to explain where they stop being valid.
  • Write clean, tested Python and review other people's code, including AI-written code.
  • Set up benchmarks and regression tests so results are reproducible.
  • Decide what to build, what to reuse (for example open-source solvers and OpenCascade) and what to leave out.
  • Lead a small team, hire well, and explain technical limits to non-experts and customers.

Strong fit

PhD (or close to finishing) in computational engineering, computational mechanics or a related field, with working knowledge of CAE, FEA and CFD, who has written solver or analysis code and has shipped software that other people use. Equally welcome: engineers from CAE software companies who built production solvers, or mechanical engineers with strong coding skills who have built standards-based calculation tools.

Nice to have

DFM knowledge, experience at a CAE/CAD company, open-source simulation contributions (for example CalculiX, OpenFOAM, FEniCS), aerospace structures and aerodynamics, tolerance analysis.

How you will use AI

Use AI to speed up code, tests and literature review, and to explore solver options. Never let an AI decide an engineering number without a solver and a benchmark behind it.
